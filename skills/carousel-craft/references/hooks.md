# Hooks

The cover has one job: make the right reader swipe. It promises something the rest of the carousel delivers. A hook that over-promises costs trust and saves.

## Method

1. Name the reader and the moment (who, doing what, wanting what).
2. List the angles that fit the topic (see below). Skip angles the facts do not support.
3. Write one cover headline per fitting angle. Aim for the brand's `max_headline_words` or fewer.
4. Test each: could a competitor post it unchanged? If yes, add something specific.
5. Pick one. Keep the others as alternates for sibling carousels in the batch.

## The six angles

### Problem
Start from a pain the reader already feels. Use their words, not jargon.
- Pattern: "[Symptom] and you do not know why."
- Check: the pain is real for this audience; you can solve it in the slides.

### Contrast
Two ways of doing the same thing with different outcomes.
- Pattern: "Same budget, different result: what changed."
- Check: both sides are fair; the comparison is not rigged.

### Number
A real figure that changes how the reader sees the topic.
- Pattern: "[Figure] says [surprising consequence]."
- Check: figure, source and date are in hand. No source, no number hook. Use another angle.
- A count of items ("3 checks") is allowed without a source because it describes your own structure.

### Mistake
A common wrong move and its cost, described without invented figures.
- Pattern: "The [adjective] mistake in [task]."
- Check: you can show the fix on slide 3 or 4.

### Myth
A widely held belief that does not hold up.
- Pattern: "[Belief]? Not quite."
- Check: the belief is actually common; your correction has evidence or a clear argument.

### How-to
The result first, then the effort.
- Pattern: "How to [result] in [N] steps."
- Check: the steps fit the slide budget, one per slide.

## Cover rules

- One promise per cover. No second clause that argues with the first.
- No bait the carousel does not pay off.
- Respect the brand profile: no avoid-list words, no prices when forbidden, no exclamation marks if the brand bans them, sentence case or the case it sets.
- Use at most one accent phrase.
- If a hook feels clever but needs explaining, drop it.

## Audit questions

- Can the target reader tell in two seconds that this is for them?
- What do they gain by swiping?
- Would you say this sentence out loud to a client?
- Is every claim in it sourced?

## Batch variety

When writing N carousels for one topic, vary the angle across them (not only the wording). Two covers from the same angle with different adjectives are one idea posted twice.
